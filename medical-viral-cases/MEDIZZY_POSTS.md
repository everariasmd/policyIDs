### 1. Maggots living inside a man's ear ⚠️
`images/medizzy/01_aural_myiasis.jpg`. Original: NEJM Images in Clinical Medicine, via [MEDizzy](https://journal.medizzy.com/aural-myiasis-in-a-64-year-old/)

> 🪰 He complained of an itchy, bleeding ear for 5 days…
> Doctors looked inside and found it was ALIVE. 😳
>
> A 64-year-old man from a rural community had his ear canal completely blocked by **moving fly larvae (maggots)**. This is **aural myiasis**: a fly lays its eggs in the ear and the larvae feed on the tissue.
>
> They were removed one by one with suction, forceps and irrigation, but they had already **perforated his eardrum**.
>
> ⚠️ Risk factors: chronic ear infections/discharge, diabetes, poor hygiene, rural/animal exposure, and elderly or bedbound patients.
> ✅ He recovered within a week with antibiotics.
>
> 👉 Never ignore a smelly, draining ear. Would you have guessed this? 👇
> #ENT #Myiasis #MedicalCases #Parasites
>
> 📷 NEJM Images in Clinical Medicine / via MEDizzy

### 2. The X-ray that left doctors speechless 🏋️
`images/medizzy/02_dumbbell_xray.png`. Original: International Journal of Surgery Case Reports, via [MEDizzy](https://journal.medizzy.com/an-unusual-case-of-rectal-foreign-body/)

> 🏋️ Leg day… went VERY wrong.
>
> A 54-year-old man came to hospital with abdominal pain, nausea and inability to pass stool, and waited 2 days before coming in. He wouldn't say why.
>
> The X-ray said it for him: a **2 kg, 20 cm DUMBBELL** lodged where the colon meets the rectum. 😳
>
> The surgeon had to remove it **manually, using his forearm**. Happily, the patient went home 3 days later with no lasting damage.
>
> 🩺 Rectal foreign bodies are more common than you think, and the delay in seeking help is what makes them dangerous (perforation, infection). Doctors have seen it all. Go early, no judgment.
>
> Caption this X-ray 👇😂
> #Radiology #XRay #ForeignBody #ERstories

### 3. A kidney… no, BLADDER stone with tree rings 🌳
`images/medizzy/04_bladder_stone_tree_rings.png`. Original: NEJM, via [MEDizzy](https://journal.medizzy.com/case-of-bladder-stones/)

> 🌳 This isn't a tree trunk. It's a CT scan of a **bladder stone**, and you can count its "rings."
>
> A 72-year-old woman had lived with **paraplegia for 30 years**. Her bladder never emptied completely (neurogenic bladder), so stagnant urine plus repeated infections let minerals settle **layer by layer, for years**.
>
> Each ring = a period of growth, just like a tree. 🤯
>
> 🩺 Lesson: people with spinal cord injury need regular bladder care and screening. Stones like this can grow silently.
> #Radiology #Urology #CTscan #MedEd #SpinalCordInjury
>
> 📷 NEJM Images in Clinical Medicine / via MEDizzy

### 4. Her veins turned BLACK after chemo
`images/medizzy/05_serpentine_hyperpigmentation.jpg`. Original: NEJM, via [MEDizzy](https://journal.medizzy.com/serpentine-supravenous-hyperpigmentation/)

> 🖤 This is NOT a tattoo. Those dark "rivers" trace her veins.
>
> A 58-year-old woman began chemotherapy (gemcitabine + docetaxel) through IV lines in her hands. Six weeks later, dark streaks appeared and **traveled up her arms, following the veins exactly**.
>
> ✅ Diagnosis: **Serpentine supravenous hyperpigmentation**, a rare reaction where chemo injected into a vein darkens the skin directly above it.
>
> It's harmless, needs no treatment, and usually fades over months.
>
> Have you or a loved one seen this during chemo? 👇
> #Oncology #Dermatology #Chemotherapy #MedicalCases
>
> 📷 NEJM Images in Clinical Medicine / via MEDizzy

### 5. Black "grains" pouring out of a foot ⚠️
`images/medizzy/07_madura_foot.jpg`. Original: Journal of Medical Case Reports (BMC, **CC BY 4.0, free to reuse with credit**), via [MEDizzy](https://journal.medizzy.com/madurella-mycetomatis-of-the-foot/)

> 🦶 Every one of those holes was draining tiny BLACK GRAINS.
>
> A 64-year-old man had this nodule come back on his foot after 10 years. Diagnosis: **Mycetoma ("Madura foot")** caused by the fungus *Madurella mycetomatis*.
>
> The fungus enters through a tiny thorn prick or cut (often from walking barefoot) and slowly eats through skin, muscle and even bone. The black grains are fungal colonies. 😳
>
> It's a WHO-listed neglected tropical disease. Untreated, it can mean amputation.
> ✅ Treatment: long courses of antifungals plus surgery. This patient healed completely.
>
> 👟 Wear shoes. Share to spread awareness. 🌍
> #Mycetoma #NTDs #Dermatology #GlobalHealth
>
> 📷 Journal of Medical Case Reports / CC BY 4.0 / via MEDizzy

> 🩺 Doctors' note: retained bullet fragments can cause infection and even **lead poisoning** over time. Every gunshot wound needs an X-ray and proper care.
