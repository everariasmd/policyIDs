## 1. He lived inside this machine for over 70 YEARS 🫁
📷 `01_man_in_the_iron_lung.jpg` · via MEDizzy · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/man-locked-inside-an-iron-lung-for-decades/)
**Hook B:** *"Polio paralyzed him at 6. He became a lawyer — from inside a metal tube."*
```
At 6 years old, he caught polio.
He spent the next 70+ YEARS breathing inside this metal tube. 🫁😳

It was 1952. Polio paralyzed him from the neck down.
He couldn't breathe on his own — doctors almost gave up on him.

They put him in an "IRON LUNG": a sealed tank that changes air pressure around the body to pull air into the lungs.

.
.
.

👉 Most polio patients needed an iron lung for weeks or months.
Paul Alexander never fully left his — and refused to let it stop him.

✔️ He taught himself "frog breathing" — gulping air with his throat muscles — to leave the machine for short periods
✔️ He finished school and earned a LAW degree
✔️ He worked as a lawyer
✔️ He wrote a memoir — typing with a stick held in his mouth

He was recognized by Guinness as the person who lived longest in an iron lung, and died in 2024, aged 78. 🕊️

🤯 Iron lungs stopped being made decades ago — keeping his running depended on rare parts and volunteers who knew how to repair them.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: in the 1950s, hospital wards were filled with rows of iron lungs — until the polio vaccine arrived.
🩺 Polio has no cure. The vaccine is the only reason we don't see wards like that today. 💉

What would you have done in his place? 💬

Look at the photo on his ID card. Now look at his face.
SAME man. Just weeks apart. 🪪😳

The 76-year-old was admitted to hospital feeling weak.
Staff couldn't recognize him from his ID — his skin had turned a deep blue-grey all over his body.

.

.

👉 It was CANCER — showing on the outside.

He had melanoma (a skin cancer) that had spread to his liver and spleen.
In rare cases, the cancer cells release so much pigment into the body that ALL the skin slowly darkens to blue-grey.
It's called melanosis cutis.

🤯 It happens in only about 1–2% of people with advanced melanoma — and even the urine can turn dark.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: melanoma is the skin cancer most known for spreading — but it's highly curable when caught early.
🩺 Check your moles with the ABCDE rule: Asymmetry, Border, Color, Diameter, Evolving. Anything changing = see a doctor.

## 3. His blood turned the color of MILK 🥛🩸
📷 `03_milky_white_blood.jpg` · Original: case report (German hospital) · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/blood-so-thick-it-changed-to-the-colour-of-milk/)
**Hook B:** *"His blood was so thick it clogged the hospital's machine. Twice."*
```
These are blood tubes from ONE man.
That white layer isn't milk — it's FAT. 🥛😳

A 39-year-old in Germany came to the ER with vomiting, headaches and growing confusion.

His blood was so loaded with fat (triglycerides) that it had turned creamy-white.

.
.
.

👉 The normal treatment is a machine that filters fat out of the blood (plasmapheresis).
But his blood was so THICK it clogged the machine's filter. Twice. 😳

So doctors turned to a treatment from ancient history: BLOODLETTING.
They removed about 2 liters of blood, replacing it with fluids — until the fat level dropped enough for the machine to work.

✅ By day 5, his confusion and other brain symptoms were gone.

🤯 The doctors said it was the first time they knew of this ancient method being used this way.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: after a fatty meal, everyone's blood gets slightly cloudy for a few hours — this was that, times a thousand.
🩺 Very high triglycerides can cause pancreatitis. Unexplained weight loss and headaches = get blood tests.

Did you know blood could turn white? 💬

#Cholesterol #Triglycerides #MedicalCases

## 4. Her blood came out BLUE-BROWN, from a toothache gel 🦷
📷 `04_chocolate_blue_blood.jpg` · Original: NEJM 2019 (Rhode Island) · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/can-blood-be-blue/)
**Hook B:** *"Her blood was full of oxygen. Her body couldn't use any of it."*
```
Look at the blood in these syringes. It's DARK BLUE-BROWN. 🩸😳

A 25-year-old woman had a bad toothache.
She used a LOT of numbing gel (benzocaine) the night before.

The next day she was weak, short of breath — and her skin had turned bluish.
Oxygen didn't help.

When doctors drew her blood, it was this color.

.
.
.

👉 Diagnosis: METHEMOGLOBINEMIA.

The numbing chemical changed the iron in her blood, so 44% of her hemoglobin couldn't carry oxygen properly.
Her blood LOOKED full of oxygen on some tests… but her body couldn't use it.

✅ The antidote? An injection of METHYLENE BLUE — a blue dye. Her color returned and her symptoms fully resolved. 🙌

🤯 Using a blue dye to fix "blue" blood sounds made up — but it's real.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: the blood in your veins isn't blue — veins only LOOK blue because of how light passes through skin.
🩺 Never use more numbing gel than the label says — and don't use benzocaine teething gels on babies.

Did you know a toothache gel could do this? 💬

#Toothache #Toxicology #DidYouKnow

🤯 The tumor was about 30 cm across — wider than his own head.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: not all brain tumors stay inside the skull — some grow outward through the bone.
🩺 A lump on the head that keeps growing — or headaches with vision changes — needs urgent scans.

## 10. In 1518, 400 people danced until some reportedly DIED 💃☠️
📷 `10_dancing_plague_1518.jpg` · Historical painting (public domain era) · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/a-group-of-400-people-practically-danced-themselves-to-death/)
**Hook B:** *"One woman started dancing in the street. She didn't stop for 6 days."*
```
In July 1518, a woman in Strasbourg started dancing in the street.
She didn't stop for DAYS. 💃😳

Within a week, dozens of others had joined her.
Within a month: around 400 people were dancing — uncontrollably, day and night.

.
.
.

👉 This was the DANCING PLAGUE of 1518.

Doctors of the time blamed "hot blood."
Their cure? Build a stage and hire MUSICIANS so people could "dance it out." 🎻
It made things worse — more people joined.

Some dancers are reported to have collapsed and died from exhaustion, though historians still debate how many.

🤯 To this day, no one knows exactly what caused it.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: the leading theory is "mass psychogenic illness" — extreme stress (famine and disease) spreading through a community like a contagion. Another theory blames ergot, a hallucinogenic mold on rye bread.
🩺 Stress can cause real physical symptoms — the mind and body are more connected than we think.
