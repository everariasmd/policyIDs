# What makes medical case images go viral on Facebook

## Evidence (what the research actually shows)
- **Images beat everything else.** Health and medical Facebook pages see far more engagement on photo posts than on text, links or video. One diabetes page saw ~4x the likes and ~11x the shares, and one research institute saw ~5.2x the engagement.
- **Case images and quizzes boost reach.** A radiology-education Facebook page saw significantly higher reach for case images, illustrations and quiz-style posts.
- **Negative or unsettling emotion drives comments.** Negative-tone health posts drew about 3x the comments (comments are what Facebook's ranking rewards most).
- **"Weird but real" crosses over to the general public.** NEJM's "Black Hairy Tongue" (2018) was picked up by the Washington Post and went global. The pattern: an unbelievable image, a simple cause, and a happy ending.
- **Visible medical equipment helps.** Posts showing medical equipment were more likely to outperform on Instagram.

## The viral formula for this page
1. **Hook in the first line** ("This is NOT a tattoo.", "Doctors pulled THIS out of a 14-year-old.")
2. **Quiz / guess first:** "Diagnosis? Answer in comments 👇". This drives the comments the algorithm rewards. Post the answer as the first comment or edit it in after 2 to 4 hours.
3. **Short story:** age, symptom, twist.
4. **One takeaway or lesson** keeps it educational. That protects you under Meta policy and builds authority.
5. **Payoff or resolution** ("tongue returned to normal in 4 weeks"). People share happy endings.
6. **3 to 5 hashtags max**, for example #MedEd #Radiology #ClinicalImages #MedicalCases #FOAMed.

**Formats that work best:** "Guess the diagnosis", "X-ray vs. photo" side-by-side (carousel), "What happened next…", "Rare 1-in-a-million".

## Staying Facebook-compliant (Meta Violent & Graphic Content policy)
- **Medical context is protected,** but organs, open wounds or surgery may get a **warning screen**, which cuts reach. Prefer:
  - X-rays, CT and ultrasound (almost never flagged, and very shareable)
  - Skin, tongue, eye or nail findings
  - Removed specimens on a tray (worms, stones, hairballs). These sometimes get a warning screen, so test them.
- **Avoid:** open surgical fields with blood, burns, deceased persons, children's faces, genitals or nudity.
- **Ads and boosts are stricter.** Do NOT boost posts showing innards, worms or foreign objects in skin. Ads can't include those.
- **Never** use fear or misinformation ("this cure doctors hide…"). Health-misinformation strikes kill pages.

## Legal and ethical checklist (important)
- Use only **openly licensed** images (CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0, public domain) or your own **consented, de-identified** cases.
- **Credit the author and license in the caption** or the first comment. Required for CC BY and CC BY-SA.
- Do NOT take images from NEJM, Radiopaedia (CC BY-NC-SA bars monetized pages), or other pages without permission. Copyright strikes get pages unpublished.
- For your own patients: written consent, and crop out faces, tattoos, IDs, dates and hospital names.
