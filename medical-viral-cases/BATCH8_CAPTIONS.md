# Batch 8: research-upgraded captions (v3) · images in `images/batch8/`

## What the research says, and how these captions apply it

| Research finding | How the v3 captions use it |
|---|---|
| **High-arousal emotions get shared most**: awe, surprise, anxiety and anger. Low-arousal sadness gets shared less (Berger & Milkman, ~7,000 NYT articles; health stories that were surprising or awe-inspiring stood out). | Every caption has an **awe beat** ("The human body is wild…") and a **surprise twist**. Sad endings are framed with a protective lesson, not left as pure sadness. |
| **Disgust alone did NOT predict sharing** in that data. | Gross images are paired with **awe or practical value**, never "gross for gross's sake". |
| **STEPPS** (Berger's model of why things spread): **S**ocial currency, **T**riggers, **E**motion, **P**ublic, **P**ractical value, **S**tories. | 🧠 **"Fact to impress your friends"** line (social currency) · tied to **everyday triggers** (your dog, mosquitoes, tonsils, smoking) · **story-first** structure · one **share-worthy safety tip** (practical value). |
| **Meta ranks with explicit signals** like comments and reshares, and demotes engagement bait ("tag a friend", "comment YES", "share if"). | Endings are **opinion or experience questions**, which are easier to answer than knowledge questions. No tag, share or vote bait. |
| **The hook decides everything**: only about the first 90 to 125 characters show on mobile, and number and contradiction hooks are the most-recommended formats. | Line 1 = **specific number + contradiction**, under ~90 characters. Line 2 = **pattern interrupt** that deepens the mystery. |
| **Short lines and scannability.** | One idea per line, about 120 to 180 words, the answer hidden below dots. |

**v3 template:**
```
[HOOK: number + contradiction, <90 chars]
[Pattern interrupt: 1 line that deepens the mystery]

[Story: 3–5 short lines, present tense, sensory details]
.
.
.
👉 [Reveal]
[1–2 lines of mechanism, written for a 12-year-old]

🤯 [Awe beat]
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: [one surprising, repeatable fact]
🩺 [One practical tip people will want to pass on]

[Opinion/experience question]
#Tag1 #Tag2 #Tag3
📷 Credit
```

Sources: Berger & Milkman, *What Makes Online Content Viral?* ([PDF](https://cssh.northeastern.edu/pandemic-teaching-initiative/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2020/09/What-Makes-Online-Content-Viral.pdf)); [Scientific American](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/the-secret-to-online-success-what-makes-content-go-viral/); [Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/?p=10141); [Meta Transparency: ranking](https://transparency.meta.com/kn-in/features/ranking-and-content/); [Hootsuite: Facebook algorithm](https://blog.hootsuite.com/facebook-algorithm/); [ActiveToast: hook formulas](https://www.activetoast.com/post/7-hook-formulas-that-will-still-work-in-2026-and-how-to-adapt-them); [SocialPilot: caption length](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/ideal-social-media-caption-length).

🤯 Porcupine quills have tiny backward-facing barbs — once in, they keep moving DEEPER, not out.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: a porcupine has about 30,000 quills.
🩺 Dog vs porcupine? Let a vet remove the quills — and keep your hands away from your mouth.

✅ She went home 3 weeks after surgery with no complications.

```
This came out of a 12-year-old's TONSIL. It's 3.9 cm long. 🪨

For a YEAR, swallowing hurt.
For 6 years, she kept getting sore throats.
Doctors planned to take her tonsils out.

But on surgery day, there was a hard white patch on her left tonsil that hadn't been there before.

.
.
.

👉 An X-ray and CT showed a giant TONSIL STONE (tonsillolith) — almost 4 cm.

Tonsils have little pockets. Food bits, dead cells and bacteria collect there and slowly harden like cement.
Most tonsil stones are the size of a rice grain. This one grew to nearly the size of a golf ball.

✅ Surgeons removed the stone, then the tonsils. She recovered without problems.

🤯 Many people cough up tiny tonsil stones and never know what they are.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: tonsil stones are a common hidden cause of bad breath.
🩺 Gargling and good oral hygiene help stop them forming.

Be honest — have you ever coughed one up? 😅

## 3. She watched a worm crawl ACROSS her eye 👁️
📷 `03_eye_worm_loa_loa.jpg` · Original: NEJM · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/woman-presented-with-moving-worm-like-structure-in-eyelid/)
**Hook B:** *"Something was moving under the surface of her eye."*
```
She came back from Africa. Weeks later, something was MOVING in her eye. 👁️🪱

A 36-year-old anthropologist had just worked in the Central African Republic.
Then she saw it: a worm-like shape sliding under the surface of her eye and eyelid.

Her blood test was even more shocking 👇

.
.
.

👉 Under the microscope: 3,400 tiny baby worms in every milliliter of her blood (photos B & C).

Diagnosis: LOIASIS — the "African eye worm" (Loa loa).
It's spread by the bite of a deerfly (also called a mango fly).
The adult worms wander under the skin… and are sometimes seen crossing the eye. 😳

✅ She was treated with anti-parasitic medicine for 20 days. 6 months later, no sign of the worms.

🤯 The adult worms can live inside a person for years.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: around 29 million people in Central and West Africa are at risk.
🩺 After travel, ANY "moving" sensation under the skin or eye = see a doctor and mention where you went.

Would you go back to the rainforest after this? 🌍

A 55-year-old mechanic became forgetful… impulsive… saying inappropriate things in public.

The scans showed this 👇 (the big round shape at the front of the brain)

.

.

👉 A GIANT ANEURYSM — a blood vessel wall that had ballooned out to 7 cm. The size of a peach.

It was pressing on his frontal lobe — the part of the brain that controls personality and behavior.
Doctors believe it had been growing for about 10 YEARS.
His risk factors: smoking, and a sister who had died from a brain aneurysm.

✅ After a 23-hour surgery, his symptoms improved — and he went back to work 3 months later. 💪

🤯 Surgeons described it as the largest reported aneurysm of that artery.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: a sudden personality change in an adult always deserves a medical check — it can be the brain, not "just behavior."
🩺 A family history of brain aneurysm? Ask your doctor about screening — and don't smoke.

## 5. A tapeworm cyst was pushing her eye OUT 👁️
📷 `05_tapeworm_cyst_behind_eye_mri.jpg` · Original: NEJM · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/a-rare-presentation-of-hydatid-cyst/)
**Hook B:** *"Look at her left eye on the MRI. What's behind it?"*
```
Look at the eyeball on the right of this MRI. Something is pushing it FORWARD. 👁️

A 31-year-old woman's left eye was bulging more and more over 2 weeks.
Her vision was blurry.
She couldn't move that eye outward.

The MRI showed a round, fluid-filled ball sitting right behind the eyeball…

.
.
.

👉 A TAPEWORM CYST (hydatid cyst).

People catch it by accidentally swallowing tapeworm eggs from DOG feces — on unwashed hands, vegetables or water.
The larvae travel through the blood and form cysts — usually in the liver or lungs.
Behind the eye? Only about 1% of cases.

Under the microscope, the cyst was full of tiny tapeworm heads with hooks. 🪝

✅ Surgeons removed the cyst. After 3 months of anti-parasitic tablets, her vision fully recovered.

🧠 Fact to impress your friends: one cyst can contain thousands of tapeworm "heads."
🩺 Deworm your dogs and wash your hands after playing with them. 🐕🧼

Do you deworm your dog regularly? 💬

🤯 You can live without a spleen — but you're more vulnerable to certain infections afterwards.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: HIV medication taken consistently can make the virus undetectable — and protect the immune system.
🩺 Never stop HIV treatment without talking to your doctor.

## 8. His skin CRACKLED like bubble wrap 🫧
📷 `08_air_under_skin_xray.jpg` · Original: case report (PMC6286736) · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/acute-airway-compromise-due-to-subcutaneous-emphysema/)
**Hook B:** *"Those dark streaks along his chest and neck? That's AIR under his skin."*
```
Press on his chest and it CRACKLES — like bubble wrap. 🫧

A 35-year-old man came in 2 days after being assaulted.
His chest and neck were swelling up more and more.

Look at the X-ray: see the dark streaks along the sides of his chest and up into his neck (arrows)?

.
.
.

👉 That's AIR — trapped UNDER HIS SKIN.

Multiple broken ribs, and a collapsed lung.
Air leaked out of the lung… and spread through the tissue under the skin, up into his neck. This is "subcutaneous emphysema."

The scary part: the swelling started squeezing his AIRWAY. Doctors had to put in a breathing tube.

✅ The air slowly absorbed over 2 weeks, and he went home on day 28.

🤯 Doctors can literally FEEL this condition — the skin crackles under your fingers.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: the medical name for that crackling is "crepitus."
🩺 After a chest injury: swelling, crackly skin, or trouble breathing = emergency.

Had you ever heard of air getting trapped under the skin? 💬

## 9. Worms up to 60 cm were coming OUT of his legs 🪱
📷 `09_mystery_worm_vietnam.jpg` · Original: *International Journal of Infectious Diseases* · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/man-in-vietnam-contracts-mystery-worm-infection/)

```
Worms up to 60 cm long were coming out of this man's arms and legs.
And scientists didn't even know what species they were. 🪱😳

A 23-year-old in Vietnam had fevers, fatigue, muscle pain — and abscesses on his limbs with WORMS hanging out of them.
He went to several clinics. No one could diagnose him.

.
.
.

👉 At a tropical disease hospital, doctors pulled out FIVE adult worms — 30 to 60 cm each.

They looked like the infamous Guinea worm… but were too short.
Samples went to the CDC for DNA testing.
Result: an UNKNOWN species of Dracunculus — closest to worms found in reptiles. 🦎

The likely source? His diet of raw vegetables and fresh river fish.

✅ Doctors removed the worms and gave him 4 weeks of anti-parasitic treatment.

🤯 Guinea worm disease has been cut by more than 99% worldwide — and could become only the second human disease ever eradicated (after smallpox).
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: the "Rod of Asclepius" medical symbol may come from how Guinea worms were pulled out — wound slowly around a stick.
🩺 Boil or filter river water. Cook fish thoroughly.

Would you eat raw river fish after reading this? 🐟

## 10. Her hip muscle turned into BONE 🦴
📷 `10_muscle_turned_to_bone_ct.jpg` · via MEDizzy · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/myositis-ossificans/)
**Hook B:** *"Weeks in bed. Then a piece of bone grew where muscle should be."*
```
She spent weeks in bed recovering from an infection. Then her muscle started turning into BONE. 🦴

A 40-year-old woman in Korea was bedbound for weeks while recovering from tuberculous meningitis (TB of the brain's lining).
Then her right hip became very painful — her leg stuck bent and turned outward.

Her first X-ray: normal.
One month later, the CT showed this 👇 (arrows)

.
.
.

👉 A chunk of real, mature BONE growing INSIDE her buttock muscle.

It's called MYOSITIS OSSIFICANS — the body mistakenly builds bone in soft tissue.
It's usually after an injury… but it can also happen after long periods of immobility or serious illness.

🤯 Early on, it doesn't show on X-ray at all — which is why it's easy to miss.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: there's a rare genetic disease (FOP) where muscles gradually turn into bone throughout the body — sometimes called "stone man syndrome."
🩺 For anyone bedbound: gentle movement and physiotherapy matter more than people think.

Have you ever had a recovery that took way longer than expected? 💬
