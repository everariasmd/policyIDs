## 1. 14,000 volts turned his eye lenses into STARS ⭐
📷 `01_star_shaped_cataracts.jpg` · Original: NEJM 2016 (UC San Diego) · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/electric-burn-star-shaped-cataract/)
**Hook B:** *"Look closely at his pupils. Those stars weren't there before the accident."*
```
Look inside his pupils. See the STAR shapes? ⭐😳

An electrician in California touched a damaged wire with his shoulder.
14,000 volts went through his body.

He survived.
But 4 weeks later, his vision started getting cloudy.

.
.
.

👉 The electricity had damaged the lenses of his eyes — creating star-shaped CATARACTS in both eyes.

Surgeons replaced both cloudy lenses with artificial ones.
His vision improved… but not completely. The current had also damaged his optic nerves — the "cables" from the eye to the brain.

As his doctor put it: "The eye is like a camera. You can replace the lens, but if the film is damaged, you'll never get a good picture."

🤯 Electrical cataracts can appear weeks, months, or even years after a shock.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: the optic nerve conducts signals like a wire — which is exactly why electricity can damage it.
🩺 After any serious electric shock, get an eye exam — even if you feel fine.

10 years later, he still has reduced vision — but he takes public transport and studies at college. 💪

Would you have survived 14,000 volts? ⚡

#Ophthalmology #ElectricalSafety #Survivor

## 2. His scalp looks like a BRAIN 🧠
📷 `02_brain_like_scalp.jpg` · Image: Elsevier (via MEDizzy) · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/mounds-ridges-and-valleys-on-the-scalp-spot-diagnosis/)
**Hook B:** *"No, it's not a hairstyle. It's his skin."*
```
No, that's not a haircut or a hat.
That's his actual SCALP. 🧠😳

A 30-year-old man had deep folds and ridges across the top and back of his head — since puberty.
They were soft, spongy… and didn't flatten when pressed.

All his blood tests were normal.

.
.
.

👉 It's CUTIS VERTICIS GYRATA — a rare condition where the scalp skin thickens and folds into ridges and valleys, like the surface of a brain.

It's also nicknamed "bulldog scalp." 🐶

This type usually affects men after puberty — doctors think testosterone plays a role.
Hair is often thinner on the ridges and normal in the grooves (you can see it in the photo).

🤯 In some people it's linked to other conditions — but in his case, there was no other cause.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: "gyrata" refers to the "gyri" — the folds of the brain it resembles.
🩺 The folds need gentle cleaning to avoid infections and odor. Surgery can flatten them for people who want it.

What did you think it was at first? 👀

#Dermatology #RareDisease #ClinicalImages

## 3. Lightning left a FERN-shaped pattern on his arm ⚡🌿
📷 `03_lightning_scar_pattern.jpg` · via MEDizzy · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/an-unbelievable-case-of-a-lightning-strike-survivor/)
**Hook B:** *"He went out in a storm to save his pumpkins. Lightning signed his arm."*
```
He went outside during a storm to save his pumpkins. 🎃⚡
This is what he came back with.

The 24-year-old saw a flash and heard a bang in the yard next door.
He felt fine and walked back inside.

30–45 minutes later, THIS appeared on his arm. 😳

.
.
.

👉 It's called a LICHTENBERG FIGURE — the "lightning flower."

When lightning travels over the skin, it leaves this branching, fern-like pattern — likely from the current traveling along tiny blood vessels near the surface.

The next day he had blisters, and he had random pains down his arm for over a month.

🤯 The irony: he's an ELECTRICIAN. ⚡
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: about 9 out of 10 people struck by lightning survive — but many have long-term nerve problems.
🩺 "When thunder roars, go indoors." If you can hear thunder, you're close enough to be struck.

Would you have gone outside for the pumpkins? 🎃

## 4. Doctors found 27 CONTACT LENSES stuck in her eye 👁️
📷 `04_27_contact_lenses.jpg` · Original: BMJ (via MEDizzy) · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/doctors-find-27-contact-lenses-stuck-in-67-year-old-womans-eye/)
**Hook B:** *"She thought the irritation was just getting older."*
```
She went in for routine cataract surgery.
Surgeons found 27 CONTACT LENSES hiding in her eye. 👁️😳

The 67-year-old had felt something strange in her right eye for years.
She thought it was just aging.

Before surgery, doctors spotted a hard, bluish lump under her upper eyelid…

.
.
.

👉 It was 17 contact lenses, stuck together with mucus into one mass.
Then they found 10 MORE. (That's them in the photo.)

She had worn monthly disposable lenses for 35 years.
Doctors think her deep-set eyes and poor eyesight meant she didn't notice when lenses slipped up under the lid instead of falling out.

✅ Her surgery was delayed because of the infection risk, but later went ahead without complications.

🤯 A "lost" contact lens can slide up behind the upper eyelid and stay there.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: a contact lens CAN'T get behind your eye — the inner lining of the eyelid blocks it. It can only get stuck under the lid.
🩺 Lost a lens and the eye still feels odd? Get it checked. And never sleep in lenses unless they're made for it.

Contact lens wearers — have you ever lost one in your eye? 👀

## 5. A 10-year-old fell onto a meat skewer, and it went THROUGH his head 😳
📷 `05_skewer_through_head_xray.jpg` · via MEDizzy (University of Kansas Health System case) · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/boy-survives-after-a-meat-skewer-pierces-his-head/)

```
This X-ray belongs to a 10-year-old boy. Yes, that's a metal skewer. 😳

He was playing in a treehouse when he was attacked by wasps.
In a panic, he fell off the ladder…
straight onto a rotisserie skewer lying on the ground.

.
.
.

👉 The skewer went in through his FACE and all the way to the back of his neck, passing just under his skull.

Somehow it missed:
✅ his brain and brainstem
✅ his carotid artery
✅ his vertebral artery
✅ every major nerve

His surgeon said: "If this was a millimeter closer, it probably would have been an unsurvivable injury."

✅ After careful surgery, the skewer was removed — and a day later he was back to playing video games, with just a bandage on his face. 🎮

🤯 Doctors called the path "one in a million."
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: surgeons never pull out an impaled object in the field — it can be the only thing stopping massive bleeding.
🩺 If someone is impaled: DON'T remove it. Keep them still, support the object, and call emergency services.

Luckiest kid alive? 🍀

## 6. The "Balloon Man": 40 pounds of stool inside one man 🎈
📷 `06_balloon_man_megacolon.jpg` · Mütter Museum specimen (via MEDizzy) · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/man-with-40-pound-manure-in-his-abdomen/)
⚠️ *Historical case, and the man died. Keep the tone respectful.*
**Hook B:** *"This is a human colon. It's 8 feet long and on display in a museum."*
```
This is a human COLON. 8 feet long.
And it's on display in a museum. 🎈😳

In the 1800s, a young man had suffered with constipation since before he was 2.
His belly grew bigger every year.
By 16, he could go a MONTH without a bowel movement.

He was eventually exhibited in a Philadelphia "freak show" as the "Balloon Man."

.
.
.

👉 He was found dead in his bathroom, straining.
His enormous colon held about 40 POUNDS of stool.

Today, his dried colon is preserved at the Mütter Museum in Philadelphia.

Doctors now know the cause: HIRSCHSPRUNG'S DISEASE — babies born without nerve cells in part of the bowel, so it can't push stool along.

🤯 It affects about 1 in 5,000 babies, and boys about 4 times more often.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: today it's usually diagnosed in newborns — often when a baby doesn't pass its first stool within 48 hours.
🩺 A baby with a swollen belly who isn't pooping = see a doctor. Surgery today is very successful.

Have you ever been to a medical museum? 🏛️

A prosthetic ear wasn't an option: there was no bone left to attach it to.

🧠 Fact to impress your friends: rib cartilage is used because it's strong, flexible — and the body won't reject its own tissue.
🩺 Check your skin — especially ears, nose and face. A sore that won't heal = see a doctor.

At 14, a badminton shuttlecock hit her in the eye. 🏸

28 years later, her upper eyelid started swelling and drooping.

The MRI showed a small cyst above her eye (red arrows)…

.
.
.

👉 Inside it: the contact lens she lost as a teenager. 😳

✅ Surgeons removed the cyst and the lens.

🤯 Only a handful of cases like this have ever been reported — this one had the longest gap.
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: hard (rigid) lenses are more likely to migrate than soft ones.
🩺 Lost a lens after a hit to the eye? Get checked — don't assume it fell out.

## 9. His hair fell out, and he was slowly being POISONED ☠️
📷 `09_thallium_poisoning_hair_loss.jpg` · Original: Liu et al., *JAMA Ophthalmology* 2015 · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/thallium-poisoning/)
**Hook B:** *"Doctors first said it was a stomach bug."*
```
First doctors said it was a stomach bug.
A month later his hair was falling out — and he was losing his memory. ☠️

A young man came to the ER with stomach pain, nausea and diarrhea. They gave him fluids and sent him home.

A month later he was back, worse:
❌ hair falling out
❌ confusion
❌ short-term memory loss
❌ vision problems

Test after test came back normal… until he mentioned where he worked: a CHEMISTRY LAB.

.
.
.

👉 His blood was full of THALLIUM — a highly toxic metal.

He was treated with PRUSSIAN BLUE — yes, the same blue pigment used in paint — which traps thallium in the gut so the body can get rid of it.

His other symptoms faded, but the poison had permanently damaged his optic nerves: lasting vision loss and color blindness. 💔

🤯 Thallium has no taste or smell — it was once called "the poisoner's poison."
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: an Agatha Christie novel, *The Pale Horse*, helped a nurse recognize a real thallium poisoning in 1977.
🩺 Unexplained hair loss + stomach problems + nerve symptoms = tell doctors about ANY chemicals at work or home.

Did you know a paint pigment could be an antidote? 🎨

#Toxicology #Poisoning #WorkplaceSafety

## 10. Count his fingers. Now find his THUMBS 🖐️
📷 `10_hands_without_thumbs.jpg` · via MEDizzy · [source](https://journal.medizzy.com/case-of-five-finger-hands-and-polydactyly-of-feet/)
**Hook B:** *"Five fingers on each hand. Zero thumbs. And 7 toes on each foot."*
```
Count the fingers on each hand: five. 🖐️
Now find the thumbs. 👀

.
.
.

👉 There aren't any.

This 22-year-old man was born with NO thumbs — instead, each hand has five finger-like digits.
And his feet? SEVEN toes on each foot.

It's a very rare combination: "five-finger hand" with extra toes (polydactyly).
His chromosomes were normal, and no one else in his family had it.

🤯 Even without thumbs, he could grip objects — by pinching between his first two fingers. The human body adapts. 💪
🧠 Fact to impress your friends: the thumb does about 40% of the work of your whole hand — which is why surgeons can turn an index finger into a thumb ("pollicization").
🩺 Babies born with extra or missing fingers should see a hand specialist early — function matters more than appearance.

Try picking something up right now WITHOUT using your thumb. How hard is it? 😅
