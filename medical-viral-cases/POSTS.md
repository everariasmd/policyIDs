### 1. Argyria: the man who turned blue
**Image:** `images/Argyria_2.jpg`
**Credit:** Herbert L. Fred, MD & Hendrik A. van Dijk, CC BY 2.0, via Wikimedia Commons ([source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Argyria_2.jpg))

> This man is NOT cold. He is NOT dead. He's BLUE. Permanently. 💙
>
> A 92-year-old man (right), shown next to a normal-skinned man, came in with slate-grey/blue skin, and he felt completely fine.
>
> The cause? 🤔 Guess before you read on 👇
>
> .
> .
> .
> ✅ **Generalized ARGYRIA.** For years he had used **nose drops containing silver**. The skin biopsy showed silver deposits in the dermis. Sunlight darkens the silver, like old photographic film.
>
> ⚠️ The color is usually **permanent**. Today's most common cause is "colloidal silver" supplements sold online as immune boosters.
>
> 🩺 Lesson: "Natural" doesn't mean safe. Always tell your doctor about every supplement you take.
>
> #Argyria #MedicalCases #Dermatology #MedEd #Toxicology
>

### 2. A bowl of worms from a 14-year-old's intestine ⚠️
**Image:** `images/Ascaris_Lumbricoides_that_had_caused_intestinal_obstruction.jpg`
**Credit:** Nmitei, CC BY-SA 4.0, via Wikimedia Commons ([source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Ascaris_Lumbricoides_that_had_caused_intestinal_obstruction.jpg))

> This is NOT spaghetti. 🍝❌
>
> Surgeons removed ALL of these from the intestine of a **14-year-old** who had a complete bowel obstruction.
>
> They are **Ascaris lumbricoides** (giant roundworm). Each one can grow to 15 to 35 cm long. A heavy load can tangle into a ball that blocks the bowel completely.
>
> 🦠 How do you get them? By swallowing microscopic eggs from soil contaminated with human feces, on unwashed hands, raw vegetables or dirty water.
>
> 🌍 Hundreds of millions of people are infected worldwide, mostly children.
>
> 💊 Usually a cheap single-dose deworming tablet treats it. Surgery is only needed when it gets THIS bad.
>
> 🧼 Prevention: handwashing, safe water, wash your vegetables, proper sanitation.
>
> Have you ever been dewormed? 👇
>
> #Ascaris #Parasites #Surgery #MedicalCases #GlobalHealth
>

### 3. The heart is on the WRONG side
**Image:** `images/Situs_inversus_chest_Nevit.jpg`
**Credit:** Nevit Dilmen, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons ([source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Situs_inversus_chest_Nevit.jpg))

> 🫀 SPOT THE PROBLEM. 99% of people miss it at first glance. 👀
>
> Look at the "R" marker (top-left = patient's RIGHT side)…
>
> .
> .
> .
> ✅ The heart is pointing to the RIGHT, and the stomach air bubble is on the right too.
>
> This is **SITUS INVERSUS**: every organ in the chest and abdomen is a mirror image of normal. It affects about **1 in 10,000 people**, and most never know until an X-ray.
>
> 😮 Fun fact: the first thing a radiologist checks is "did the tech flip the film?" The marker says no. This is the real anatomy.
>
> 🩺 Why it matters: their appendicitis pain is on the LEFT. Some have **Kartagener syndrome** (chronic sinus/lung infections + infertility).
>
> Tag someone who'd have NO idea 👇
>
> #Radiology #SitusInversus #XRay #MedEd #SpotTheDiagnosis
>
> 📷 Nevit Dilmen / CC BY-SA 3.0 / Wikimedia Commons

### 4. Black hairy tongue
[File:Black tongue.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Black_tongue.jpg). **Public domain**

> 👅 Is this tongue growing HAIR?! Sort of.
> This is **Black Hairy Tongue**. The tiny bumps on the tongue (filiform papillae) grow too long and trap bacteria, food and pigment.
> Triggers: antibiotics (in a famous NEJM case, minocycline), smoking, coffee/tea, poor oral hygiene, a soft diet.
> ✅ Harmless and reversible: stop the trigger and brush/scrape the tongue daily. In the NEJM case the tongue was normal within 4 weeks.
> Do you brush your tongue? 👇 #OralHealth #MedicalCases #Dentistry

### 5. A horn growing out of human skin
[File:Cutaneous horn.JPG](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cutaneous_horn.JPG). **Public domain**

> 🦏 Yes, humans can grow HORNS.
> A **cutaneous horn** is a hard tower of keratin, the same protein as your nails, usually on sun-exposed skin of older adults.
> ⚠️ The horn itself isn't the danger. What's at its BASE is: it can hide a wart, a pre-cancer, or **squamous cell carcinoma**.
> 🩺 Every cutaneous horn should be removed and sent for biopsy. Don't cut it off at home.
> #Dermatology #SkinCancer #MedicalCases

### 6. The "stone baby" (lithopedion). Sensitive topic, X-ray only
[File:Lithopedion 1897 xray.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lithopedion_1897_xray.jpg). **Public domain** (1897)

> 🪨 This X-ray shows a pregnancy that turned to STONE.
> A **lithopedion** forms when a pregnancy grows outside the uterus (in the abdomen), dies, and is too large for the body to absorb. The body calcifies it to protect itself from infection.
> Some women carried one for DECADES without knowing. Fewer than ~300 cases have been described in medical history.
> 📜 This radiograph is from 1897, one of the earliest uses of X-rays in medicine!
> #MedicalHistory #Radiology #RareCases

### 7. Staghorn kidney stone
[File:Staghorn Kidney Stone 08779.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Staghorn_Kidney_Stone_08779.jpg). **CC BY-SA 3.0, Nevit Dilmen**

> 🦌 This kidney stone is shaped like DEER ANTLERS.
> A **staghorn calculus** fills the whole collecting system of the kidney. It's usually caused by repeated urinary infections with urease-producing bacteria (e.g., Proteus), which build "struvite" stone.
> It can silently destroy a kidney. Treatment is surgical (PCNL) plus clearing the infection.
> 💧 Stay hydrated & treat UTIs properly! #Urology #KidneyStones #Radiology
> 📷 Nevit Dilmen / CC BY-SA 3.0 / Wikimedia Commons

### 8. Rapunzel syndrome / trichobezoar (X-ray + ultrasound)
[File:Trichobezoar im Magen 11W - RF und US - 001.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Trichobezoar_im_Magen_11W_-_RF_und_US_-_001.jpg). **CC BY-SA 4.0, Hellerhoff**

> 💇‍♀️ An 11-year-old girl had stomach pain and weight loss. Imaging showed her stomach filled with… HAIR.
> A **trichobezoar** is a hairball formed by repeatedly swallowing hair (trichophagia), often linked to trichotillomania (hair-pulling disorder). When its "tail" extends into the intestine it's called **Rapunzel syndrome**.
> Treatment: surgical removal, PLUS mental-health support so it doesn't return. 💚
> #Pediatrics #Radiology #MentalHealthMatters
> 📷 Hellerhoff / CC BY-SA 4.0 / Wikimedia Commons

### 9. Tapeworm cysts in the brain (CT)
[File:Neurocysticercosis brain CT.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Neurocysticercosis_brain_CT.jpg). **CC BY 4.0, Innocent Lule Segamwenge**

> 🧠 Those white dots are TAPEWORM LARVAE in a human brain.
> **Neurocysticercosis** is one of the leading preventable causes of epilepsy worldwide.
> 🐖 MYTH: "You get it from eating undercooked pork." ❌ Pork gives you the intestinal tapeworm. Brain cysts come from swallowing tapeworm **EGGS** from human feces (unwashed hands, contaminated food or water).
> 🧼 Handwashing prevents brain worms. Share this. #Neurology #Parasites #Epilepsy
> 📷 Innocent Lule Segamwenge / CC BY 4.0 / Wikimedia Commons

### 10. Rhinophyma: "it's NOT from alcohol"
[File:Rhinophyma 01.jpg](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Rhinophyma_01.jpg). **CC BY 2.0, M. Sand et al.**

> 👃 People call it "drinker's nose." They're WRONG.
> **Rhinophyma** is an advanced form of **rosacea**: the skin and oil glands of the nose thicken and overgrow. Alcohol can trigger flushing, but it does NOT cause rhinophyma. Many patients have never drunk at all.
> ✅ Very treatable: laser or surgical reshaping gives dramatic results.
> Did you believe this myth? 👇 #Rosacea #Dermatology #MythBusting
> 📷 M. Sand, D. Sand, C. Thrandorf et al. / CC BY 2.0 / Wikimedia Commons
