# "What's the Diagnosis?" quiz cards: highest-engagement format

Images: `images/quiz/quiz01…quiz10.jpg` (1080×1350, Facebook's 4:5 portrait size, which takes up the most room in the feed).
Extra reveal images (post in the answer comment): `reveal01_botfly_larvae.jpg`, `reveal10_nocardia_microscope.jpg`.

## Why this format wins
- **Comments are the strongest ranking signal**, and a quiz gives everyone a reason to comment (even "no idea").
- **People argue** about the answer, and replies to replies multiply reach.
- **The A/B/C/D options are burned into the image**, so it works even when people don't tap "See more".
- **The pinned answer** sends people back into the comments.
- **The format is consistent and numbered (#1, #2…)**, so followers come back for the next one.

## How to post each one (important)
1. **Post the card** with the SHORT caption below. Keep it short: the image does the work.
2. **Wait about 2 to 3 hours**, then add the **answer comment** and **pin it**. Don't reveal it in the caption.
3. **Reply to people's guesses** ("Close! 👀", "Good thinking, but…"). Every reply keeps the post in feeds.
4. Optional: **edit the caption after 24h** to add "✅ ANSWER IN PINNED COMMENT" for late viewers.

⚠️ Avoid "Comment A if…" or "Tag a friend who…": Facebook demotes that as engagement bait. A plain question ("What's your diagnosis?") is fine.

## Quiz #2: colon in the heart sac 🫀
📷 `quiz02_colon_in_heart_sac.jpg` · Original: NEJM (NEJMicm2028463)
**Caption:**
```
A 54-year-old woman had heart palpitations that got BETTER every time she passed gas. 💨🫀
This is her CT scan.

What's your diagnosis? 👇
#WhatsTheDiagnosis #Radiology
```
**Pinned answer:**
```
✅ ANSWER: D — Her COLON was inside the sac around her heart 😳

Look again: those bubbly loops wrapped under the heart are BOWEL.
15 years earlier she'd had a procedure to drain fluid from around her heart. That likely left a weak spot — and part of her colon slipped up through the diaphragm into the heart sac (pericardium).
When gas filled the colon, it pressed on her heart → palpitations. Passing gas relieved the pressure.

✅ Surgeons pulled the colon back down with keyhole surgery. A year later: no palpitations.
📷 NEJM Images in Clinical Medicine
```

## Quiz #3: the glass brain 🌋
📷 `quiz03_glass_brain.jpg` · Image: Petrone et al., PLOS ONE 2018 (CC BY 4.0); glass-brain finding: NEJM 2020
**Caption:**
```
Archaeologists found shiny black GLASS inside the skull of a young man who died 2,000 years ago. 💀

What is it? 👇
#WhatsTheDiagnosis #History #Science
```
**Pinned answer:**
```
✅ ANSWER: B — His BRAIN, turned to glass 🧠🌋

In 79 AD, Mount Vesuvius erupted and buried Herculaneum (near Pompeii).
A young man was killed by a cloud of extremely hot volcanic ash — around 500 °C.
The heat liquefied his brain, then it cooled so fast it turned to GLASS ("vitrification").

Scientists later found brain proteins — and even preserved NEURONS — inside the glass.
It's one of the only known cases of a human brain turned to glass.

📷 Petrone et al., PLOS ONE 2018 (CC BY 4.0)
```

## Quiz #4: shoulder pad sign
📷 `quiz04_shoulder_pad.jpg` · Original: NEJM
**Caption:**
```
Doctors call this the "shoulder pad sign." 🏈
There's something building up under the skin of this patient's shoulder.

What do you think it is? 👇
#WhatsTheDiagnosis #MedicalQuiz
```
**Pinned answer:**
```
✅ ANSWER: A — AMYLOID protein deposits

In amyloidosis, abnormal proteins fold the wrong way and pile up in tissues.
When they collect around the shoulder, it bulges outward — like wearing an American football shoulder pad. 🏈
It's classically seen in long-term kidney dialysis (a protein called β2-microglobulin isn't filtered out) and in some blood disorders.
It often comes with shoulder pain and carpal tunnel syndrome.

📷 NEJM Images in Clinical Medicine
```

## Quiz #6: tooth in the nose 🦷👃
📷 `quiz06_tooth_in_nose.jpg` · Original: Cureus (via MEDizzy)
**Caption:**
```
A 15-year-old girl couldn't breathe through her left nostril — and lost some of her sense of smell — for 6 months.
Nasal sprays didn't help. This is her CT.

What's the white spot (arrow)? 👇
#WhatsTheDiagnosis #Radiology #ENT
```
**Pinned answer:**
```
✅ ANSWER: B — A TOOTH growing inside her nose 🦷👃

It's called an ectopic intranasal tooth — a tooth that develops in the wrong place and erupts upward into the nasal cavity instead of into the mouth.
Extremely rare. Can be linked to trauma, infection or cleft palate — but often there's no clear cause.

✅ Removed with an endoscope through the nose. Her breathing and sense of smell came back.
```

## Quiz #7: air in the skull
📷 `quiz07_air_in_skull.jpg` · via MEDizzy
**Caption:**
```
This 19-year-old new mom had a terrible headache after giving birth.
The black spots inside her brain scan are AIR. 😳

How did it get there? 👇
#WhatsTheDiagnosis #Radiology #Anesthesia
```
**Pinned answer:**
```
✅ ANSWER: D — During her EPIDURAL for labor 💉

During a difficult epidural, the needle likely entered the space around the spinal cord, and a small amount of air was carried inside.
Air rises — so it traveled UP the spinal canal into the fluid spaces of the brain. This is called PNEUMOCEPHALUS.
It caused severe headaches and neck pain.

✅ The body absorbs the air on its own — follow-up scans showed it shrinking, and she was treated with pain relief, fluids, rest and caffeine.
🩺 It's RARE. Epidurals are very safe for most women — this is why anesthetists monitor closely.
📷 via MEDizzy
```

## Quiz #9: hairy kidney
📷 `quiz09_hairy_kidney.jpg` · Original: NEJM
**Caption:**
```
Radiologists call this the "HAIRY KIDNEY." 🤔
It was found by accident when a 62-year-old man had a scan for gallstones.

What causes it? 👇
#WhatsTheDiagnosis #Radiology
```
**Pinned answer:**
```
✅ ANSWER: C — A rare immune-cell disease: Erdheim–Chester disease

Abnormal immune cells (histiocytes) infiltrate the fat around the kidneys, creating a fuzzy, "hairy" coat on the CT (arrows).
It can squeeze the tubes that drain the kidneys.
It's extremely rare.

✅ His cells had a BRAF gene mutation, so he got a targeted drug (vemurafenib). 1 year later: no progression.
📷 NEJM Images in Clinical Medicine
```

## Quiz #10: ring spots in the brain
📷 `quiz10_brain_rings.jpg` · Original: NEJM
**Caption:**
```
An 80-year-old man being treated for blood cancer had 2 weeks of fever and growing confusion.
His first CT was normal. This MRI wasn't.

What are the ring-shaped spots? 👇
#WhatsTheDiagnosis #Neurology
```
**Pinned answer (attach `reveal10_nocardia_microscope.jpg`):**
```
✅ ANSWER: B — Brain ABSCESSES caused by bacteria (Nocardia) 🦠

Nocardia is a soil bacterium that grows in thin, branching threads (see the microscope image).
It mostly infects people with weakened immune systems — and causes only about 1–2% of brain abscesses, with a higher death rate than most.
Ring-shaped spots on MRI = pockets of infection with a rim of inflammation.

🩺 Fever + confusion in someone on cancer treatment = urgent medical review.
📷 NEJM Images in Clinical Medicine
```
